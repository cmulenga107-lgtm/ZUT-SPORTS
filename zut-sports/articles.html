01 STUDENT LIFE

Make room for class, training and rest

A good week is planned around the commitments you already have, not an ideal timetable that ignores them.

Start with the fixed points

Write down lectures, assignment deadlines, training and travel time first. Add meals and sleep before filling the remaining gaps. This makes clashes visible early and helps you avoid treating every free hour as study time.

Give study sessions a clear finish line

Choose one task for each block, such as reviewing a lecture or outlining an assignment. A specific outcome is easier to start and easier to move when training or classes change.

Communicate before a deadline becomes a crisis

If a fixture, travel day or recovery need conflicts with coursework, speak with your lecturer or coach as soon as you can. A weekly check-in helps you adjust the plan without sacrificing sleep or recovery.

02 FOOTBALL

Build a more reliable first touch

Good control starts before the ball arrives. A quick look around gives you more options for the next action.

  • Scan your surroundings before receiving and decide where you want the ball to go next.
  • Practise cushioning and directing the ball with both feet, using a partner or a wall.
  • Begin without pressure, then add a defender or a smaller space as your control improves.

03 BASKETBALL

Keep your dribble under control

A controlled dribble helps you protect the ball and keep your head up to read the court.

  • Use your fingertips rather than slapping the ball with your palm.
  • Work both hands at a comfortable pace, keeping your body between the ball and an imagined defender.
  • Once the movement feels steady, practise changing direction and speed with a partner applying light pressure.

04 VOLLEYBALL

Find a repeatable serve

A consistent toss and contact point give you a dependable starting point for serving with intent.

  • Use the same stance and pre-serve routine each time.
  • Choose a clear target area and notice where the ball lands, not only whether it clears the net.
  • Build accuracy before adding power; ask a coach to check your technique if your shoulder feels uncomfortable.

05 ATHLETICS

Get stronger through steady training

Progress is easier to sustain when training has a purpose and recovery is treated as part of the work.

  • Warm up before faster running or event-specific drills, and focus on relaxed, repeatable technique.
  • Record your sessions and how you felt so you can spot patterns over time.
  • Increase distance or intensity gradually with guidance from your coach, and take persistent pain seriously.

These are general training ideas, not a substitute for coaching or medical advice. Confirm the sports offered at ZUT and adapt each session to your experience, facilities and health.
